History

Five thousand years, still running

Varanasi, on the banks of the Ganges, is thought to be one of the oldest continuously inhabited cities on Earth — a fitting place to start.

Ahilya Ghat, Varanasi — Wikimedia Commons, CC BY-SA

Timeline

Key chapters

c. 3300 – 1300 BCE

The Indus Valley Civilisation

One of the world's earliest urban civilisations, with planned cities, drainage systems, and standardised weights, centred around the Indus river in what is now Pakistan and northwest India.

c. 1500 – 500 BCE

The Vedic Period

Sanskrit-speaking communities composed the Vedas, the foundational texts of Hindu philosophy and ritual, laying groundwork still referenced today.

322 – 185 BCE

The Maurya Empire

India's first major empire, unifying most of the subcontinent under Chandragupta Maurya and later Ashoka, who famously renounced war after the Kalinga campaign and spread Buddhist teaching across Asia.

320 – 550 CE

The Gupta Empire

Often called India's "Golden Age" — a period of major advances in mathematics (including the concept of zero), astronomy, and Sanskrit literature.

1526 – 1857

The Mughal Empire

A Persian-influenced Muslim dynasty that ruled most of the subcontinent, leaving behind Indo-Islamic architecture including the Taj Mahal, built by Emperor Shah Jahan for his wife Mumtaz Mahal.

1858 – 1947

British Colonial Rule

Direct rule by the British Crown following the 1857 uprising, ending after a decades-long independence movement led by figures including Mahatma Gandhi, built on nonviolent civil disobedience.

15 August 1947

Independence

India gained independence, accompanied by the partition of British India into India and Pakistan — one of the largest mass migrations in human history.

26 January 1950

The Republic

India's constitution came into force, establishing the country as a sovereign, democratic republic — now the largest democracy in the world by population.

Very few places on Earth can point to a city that has been continuously lived in since before recorded history — and still is.
